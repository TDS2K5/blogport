## This is how my journey with Linux started... all my distro hopping shenanigans described.
![fedora](/images/linux/fedora.webp)

I used to be the average Windows user like everyone else when I first got my laptop. It was installed by default. Being a poweruser, I quickly realised the flaws with Windows 11. Even after customizing the hell out of it, I felt it was always lacking behind. It was high time for a change. 

Around this period, I discovered Virtual Machines. The concept itself feels so magical, like what do you mean I can run a computer inside my computer? Anyway I tried Ubuntu for the first time, just to practice bash commands. Ofcourse Ubuntu sucked, I didn't really like the side panel and GNOME DE. 

Next up I tried Linux Mint inside my VM. By then I was already familiar with basic BASH commands, so it felt comfortable, and somehow the experience was better than my actual Windows host! I decided this is going to be my go-to distro. Fired up my bootable USB, and now I'm onboard the Linux boat.

I used Mint for about a month and soon there was this itch to scratch. I felt this wasn't visually appealing enough. My desktop needed to look cool, with a lot more functionalities. My research led me to the KDE Plasma desktop enviroment, a piece of art that I'd cherish forever. I went for Kubuntu this time, and ended up daily driving it for quite a while. This is when I realised that Debian based distros aren't updated frequently for the sake of stability and if one needs the latest and greatest, it's better to go for a rolling release. Which led me to...

Arch was such a hyped distro which I kept hearing about everywhere in the community. I always wanted to try it, but kept postponing thinking my skills weren't enough. Ofcourse they weren't. The installation was no joke, and was quite a rollercoaster ride having to manually handle partitions, bootloader config and what not. I'll take a moment to appreciate the Arch Wiki, one of the most well-documented sites ever. Anyway I went with Hyprland window manager this time.

Surprisingly Arch turned out to be better than I expected. 1.5G of idle ram usage, and everything just worked? By everything I mean even Bluetooth, Wifi, and the microphone input problem which I had with every other distro. Also I quickly got used to the Hyprland key bindings, and loved the dynamic tiling. It was fun while it lasted. Until it didn't.

Soon I was met with college assignments and other stuff which meant I needed to get things done quickly in a short duration. And as much as I loved Arch, it needed some amount of system maintenance which I wasn't able to perform during that time. It felt more like a fancy gimmick rather than a practical solution to improve my workflow. I realised that my search for the perfect distro wasn't over yet. That's when I distro hopped again and...

Landed on Fedora with KDE. As Thanos said, "Perfectly balanced as all things should be". Bleeding edge, yet stable, it fulfilled all my requirements which I was looking for in a distro. I shook hands with it, and daily drive it till date.
